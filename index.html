Engineering and Existential Threats

Artificial intelligence, climate change, pandemics, and the erosion of democratic institutions are routinely called existential threats. Engineers design, operate, and evaluate the systems at the center of all of them. Over two days we ask where we are, and what we can do.

About the workshop

The International Network for Engineering Studies brings together scholars who study engineers and engineering — historians, sociologists, anthropologists, philosophers, engineering educators, and engineers themselves. This workshop is our annual gathering, held online so that members on several continents can take part.

Much of the public argument about existential risk has been carried by philosophers and social scientists. Engineers are rarely at that table, though they build and run the systems in question. The first day takes stock of where we are. The second turns from diagnosis to action, with colleagues from engineering practice and engineering education.
